How GutWell works

Three simple habits.
One growing picture.

The more consistently you log, the more useful your history becomes.

01

Check in daily — takes 60 seconds

Log how you feel each day — energy, mood, symptoms, and more. GutWell asks the right questions and stores everything so you don't have to.

02

Scan your meals (optional)

Photograph what you eat and describe your situation. GutWell notes what you ate, how you felt at the time, and what you were planning to do next.

03

Review your patterns over time

Your Gut Score updates daily based on your check-ins. Over time, your history gives you something to reflect on — and brings your personal patterns into view.

Your data, your questions

Challenge the analysis.
Ask your own questions.

GutWell gives you an analysis — but you are not required to accept it. If the AI misidentified your meal, got the context wrong, or gave advice that doesn't feel right, you can push back directly.

Correct the meal. Add what you actually ate. Ask what a different choice might have looked like. GutWell revises its guidance based on what you tell it — not the other way around.

You are talking to your own health data. GutWell is the interface — not the authority.

Example conversation

This looks like fried fish with a creamy dip — 5/10 for gut comfort today.
That was grilled, not fried. And I had it with yogurt, not dip.
Thanks for the correction. Grilled fish with yogurt is lighter — I'd revise that to 7/10. The yogurt is worth watching if you're sensitive to dairy.
What if I had skipped the yogurt entirely?
Without the dairy, this would be a straightforward high-protein meal — likely 8/10 for your gut today.

Guidance for real life

GutWell adapts to what you're doing next.

Most nutrition apps give the same advice regardless of your situation. GutWell considers your real-life context — not just what's on your plate.

Heading to a job interview? Driving for two hours? Going to sleep soon? About to exercise? The guidance you receive adapts to your situation, using careful language that reflects possibility rather than certainty.

GutWell does not make guarantees. It offers practical, situation-aware suggestions — the kind a knowledgeable friend might offer, not a clinical prescription.
